Foliu[m]/Blat IIIr

A contemporary English translation is presented from comparing the existing Latin, Early New High German (ENHG) and English (translation) texts for better comprehensibility. Especially since the ENHG text is difficult to understand compared to modern High German, as a profound knowledge of German (as a mother tongue) as well as already aknowledge of various dialects is required.

Of the Work of the Second (other) Day

SEcundo die dixit deus. Fiat firmamentum in medio aquaru[m]: et diuidat aquas ab aquis: vocauitq[ue] firmamentum celum.

Let us hear the prophet speak of celestial mysteries. God created a wheel-shaped1, movable firmament, in which other perceptible2 things were located. From frozen water, it solidified into a crystal and in this, the stars3 were attached.4 The axes of the sphere of heaven, to which the stars are fixed, should be two. These, the (i) northern (Boreas5) and the (ii) southern (Notus6), roll from east to west at such a speed that this would lead to the collapse of the world were it not for the planets slowing this movement in the opposite direction. Furthermore, the Creator of the world tempered the nature of the heavens with waters, so that the conflagration of the heavens would not set the lower elements ablaze.7

[...]8 Above9 the (I) nine celestial spheres, that is, (i) the seven planets, (ii) the eighth sphere, the unerring10, (iii) the ninth orbit, comprehensible by reason but not by the senses and the first among the moved bodies, it is assumed that (II) the tenth heaven is unchanging, at rest and still, since it does not participate in any movement.

Not only our people held this view, in particular the younger 'Strabo' and Bede, but also many Hebrews and furthermore some philosophers and mathematicians. It suffices to mention two who attested to this: Abraham, the greatest Spanish astrologer and Isaac, the philosopher. Here, Isaac interprets the tenth orbit as designated by Ezekiel through the sapphire in the form of a throne; the color of the sapphire symbolizes the brilliance of light and the analogy11 with a throne symbolizes immobility.12

⋅

But let us return to Moses, who separated the waters from the waters in the midst of the firmament, resulting in a threefold division of the sublunar bodies. Several are located (i) above the middle layer of air. In the uppermost part are its elements and purest fire, entirely the ether13, clean, pure14 and true15 elements. Others are located (ii) below the middle region16 of the air itself, that is, with us, where there is no pure element (and also no pure perceptible element), but exclusively mixtures17, due to the sediment-containing18 and denser part of the earthly body.19

The (iii) intermediate layer of air, also called firmament, from which birds originate, being introduced by the firmament of the heaven in flight, is the region in which the etheric20 impressions such as rain, snow, lightning, thunder, comets, or the like appear. This firmament (iii) therefore rightly separates the higher elements (i) from the lower ones (ii), not only because of its position but also because of its natural properties of different waters. Above21 it are the pure elements, below them, completely mixed in elemental simplicity22 separated23. Thus, he named this firmament, derived from koylon (concave), as the heaven24, since it encompasses all perceptible25 and invisible things.26

1: 'rotundum' and 'gescheibelt', disc-shaped.
2: 'sensibilia' and 'empfintliche'.
3: 'sidera' and 'gestirne'.
4: c.f. the 'Speculum maius' (De Beauvais, 1964, Speculum historiale, Book I, Chapter 1), which describes the physical nature of the firmament and the waters above the heavens, strongly influenced by Honorius Augustodunensis' 'Imago Mundi' (1532).
5: Greek god of the north wind and personification of winter.
6: Notus, the Greek god and personification of the south wind or nothus, illegitimate, false, mixed, hybrid.
7: from the 'Imago Mundi' (Augustudunensis, 1532, De Coelo, Book I, Chapter V), one of the most popular encyclopedias of the Middle Ages.
8: 'Etlich lerer nit allein auß den unsern sunder auch auß den hebreyschen vn[d] andern haben geglawbt', Several teachers, not only ours but also among the Hebrews and others, believed that…
9: 'Supra', previously vs. 'uber'.
10: 'inerrantem' and 'die unirrende[n]'.
11: 'similitudo' and 'gleichnus'.
12: from the 'Heptaplus' (Mirandola & Mirandola, 1601, Book II, Chapter 1), discusses the structure of the heavens and the existence of a tenth sphere (of the Empyreum or the immobile first intelligence) beyond the traditional nine spheres.
13: 'etheris'.
14: 'immixta' and 'unvermischt'.
15: 'legitima' and 'richtige'.
16: 'meditulliu[m]' and 'mitteln fürscheinenden stat'.
17: 'mixta' and 'gemischt'.
18: 'feculenta'.
19: c.f. the 'Calumniatorem Platonis' (Bessarion, 1503, Book IV, Chapter 6), a Neoplatonic interpretation of Genesis 1:6, which explains the division of the waters by a threefold division of the sublunar bodies.
20: 'sublimes'.
21: 'Sup[ra]' and 'darob'.
22: 'simplicitate' and 'slechtigkeit'.
23: 'discedunt' and 'obgesünderet'.
24: 'celum' (koylon) and 'himel'.
25: 'sensibilia' and 'empfintliche'.
26: c.f. the 'Epitoma in Almagestum Ptolemaei' (Regiomontanus, 1496, Praefatio), reflects a medieval synthesis of biblical cosmology and Aristotelian physics.
