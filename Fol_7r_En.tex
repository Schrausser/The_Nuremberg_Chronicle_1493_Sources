\hypertarget{foliumblat-viir}{%
\section{Foliu{[}m{]}/Blat VIIr}\label{foliumblat-viir}}

A \emph{contemporary} English translation is presented from comparing
the existing Latin, Early New High German (ENHG) and English
(translation) texts for better comprehensibility. Especially since the
ENHG text is difficult to understand compared to modern High German, as
a \emph{profound} knowledge of German (as a mother tongue) as well as
already aknowledge of various \emph{dialects} is required.

\hypertarget{the-first-age-of-the-world}{%
\subsection{The first age of the
world}\label{the-first-age-of-the-world}}

When the \emph{original} ancestors\footnote{`p{[}ro{]}thopare{[}n{]}tes'
  and `ersten eltern'.} had transgressed God's commandment under the
suggestion of the \emph{devil}\footnote{`suggerente diabolo' and `durch
  eingebung des teüfels'.} in the form of the serpent, God cursed them

\begin{quote}
et ait serpenti. Maledictus eris inter omnia anima{[}n{]}tia {[}et{]}
bestias terre: super pectus tuum gradieris: et terram comedes cunctis
diebus vite tue. Mulieri quoq{[}ue{[} dixit. M{[}u{]}ltiplicabo
eru{[}i{]}nas tuas: {[}et{]} co{[}n{]}ceptus tuos: in dolore paries
filios {[}et{]} s{[}u{]}b viri potestate eris: {[}et{]} ip{[}s{]}e
domi{[}n{]}abitur tibi. Ade v{[}er{]}o dixit Maledicta terra in opere
tue i{[}n{]} laboribus comedes ex ea: spinas {[}et{]} tribulos
germinabit tibi: in sudorevultus tui vesceris pane tuo: donec reuertaris
in terram de qua sumptus es. Et cu{[}m{]} feciss{[}et{]} eis deus
tunicas pelliceas eiecit eos de paradiso collocans ante illum cherubin
cum flammeo gladio: vt viam ligni vite custodiat.\footnote{`And he said
  to the serpent, Cursed are you more than every beast of the earth; on
  your breast you shall crawl, and you shall eat dust all the days of
  your life. To the woman he said, I will give you much affliction and
  many pregnancies; in pain you shall bear children, and you shall be
  subject to your husband, and he shall rule over you. And to Adam he
  said, Cursed is the ground in which you labored; through painful toil
  you shall eat food from it; thorns and thistles it shall produce for
  you; by the sweat of your brow you shall eat your food until you
  return to the ground from which you were taken. And when God had made
  them garments of skin, he drove them out of Paradise and placed
  cherubim with a flaming sword before them to guard the way to the tree
  of life.', Genesis 3:14-24 (vgl. Jiménez de Cisneros,
  \href{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-46695}{1517}).}
\end{quote}

Adam, the first human being formed from clay of the earth, was about
thirty years old when his wife was given the name \emph{Eve}. After he
ate the fruit of the forbidden tree, which his wife offered him, they
were cast out from the blissful\footnote{`voluptat{[}is{]}' and
  `wolusts'.} Paradise into the accursed land\footnote{`terram
  maledictionis' and `ertreich der verfluchung'.}, according to the
curse\footnote{`imprecationem' and `aufsatzung'.} of the Lord God. Adam
is to cultivate the earth by the sweat of his brow and feed himself on
his \emph{own} bread.

Eve too is to live in distress and bear children in pain; she whom the
Lord has adorned with incomparable splendor\footnote{`splendore' and
  `scheyn'.}. However, the envious enemy of her happiness\footnote{`felicitatis'
  and `seligkait'.} deceived her so that in feminine thoughtlessness she
tasted the fruit of the tree with wicked desire\footnote{`temerario
  ausu' and `frefellicher getürftigkeit'.} and drew her husband to her
will\footnote{`sente{[}n{]}tiam suam' and `in irn willen'.}. After
putting on the \emph{leaf apron}\footnote{`peri{[}z{]}omatibus
  folioru{[}m{]}' and `koste{[}n{]} pletter'.}, she was
exiled\footnote{`exul' and `enlend'.} from the Garden of Delights to the
field of Hebron, together with her husband. Having frequently suffered
the pains of childbirth, she aged through toil and labor and finally
died, just as the Lord had foretold.
