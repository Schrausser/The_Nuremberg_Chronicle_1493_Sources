Foliu[m]/Blat IIIv

A contemporary English translation is presented from comparing the existing Latin, Early New High German (ENHG) and English (translation) texts for better comprehensibility. Especially since the ENHG text is difficult to understand compared to modern High German, as a profound knowledge of German (as a mother tongue) as well as already aknowledge of various dialects is required.

Of the Work of the Third Day

TErcio die deus aquas sub firmamento in locum vnum co[n]gregauit [et] appareat arida: vocauitq[ue] aridam terram. Congretat[i]ones vero aquarum maria appellauit. Videns q[uia] bonum esset: ait germinet terra herbam virentem: [et] semen facientem: [et] lignum pomiferum facientem fructum iuxta gen[us] suum.

After discussing the firmament's integrity, position and order of the elements, he briefly reminds us of the confluence of waters into one place and the laws prescribed for the sea, so that it does not flood the land.1

If the earth was once invisible and is to come into sight2, it is necessary that the waters which are under the heaven or under the middle layer of the air are brought together in one place, into a common mixing basin, where they flow together according to certain laws, just as it is practically prescribed for the coasts to join together at one point to form a dam3.4

It is untrue that water is not found anywhere in remote and isolated places, since the Indian Sea is separated from the Hircanian Sea, the Hircanian Sea from the Adriatic Sea, the Adriatic Sea from the Euxiniian Sea, along with countless remote flowing springs and lakes, by weirs5. Since this is a separate and subdivided collection of sea or river waters, they should surely be gathered in one common place6.7 All, as Solomon says, tend towards the primary sea, are led to one place of the oceans and united.8

When the earth9 is then overwhelmed by the waves of the sea, it is initially not useful or practical for us, but becomes suitable for animals and also for our purposes when the sea recedes, then its fertility and bountifulness become increasingly apparent. This is also strongly illustrated by Moses when he presents it as the parent10 of herbs, fruits and trees and describes them as immediately green and blooming after the accumulation of water.11

So he placed it (the Earth) in the middle of the World, as the center, endowed with veins12 of metals such as gold, silver, bronze13, copper, tin, lead and (surpassing all) iron, immediately covered with all kinds of herbs, in their green ripeness, with herb seeds and trees with the sweetest fruits. It is also said that on that day he created the most fertile and glorious Source14, Paradise, with all kinds of woods and trees, composed of all the delights of springs, with green plains of trees bearing the most abundant fruit.15

This describes the endless, cyclical nature of the world.
