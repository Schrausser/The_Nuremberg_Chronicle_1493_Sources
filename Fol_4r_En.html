On the Work of the Fourth Day

QUarto die dixit deus. Fia[n]t luminaria in firmame[n]to celi: [et] diuidant diem et nocte[m].
Et sint in signa [et] t[em]p[er]a: [et] dies: [et] a[n]nos: vt lucea[n]t in firmame[n]to celi: [et] illumine[n]t terra[m].
Et factu[m] e[st] ita.
Fecitq[ue] deus duos luminaria magna: luminare maius: vt p[re]esset diei: [et] luminare min[us]: vt p[er]esset nocti [et] stellas: vt duiderent luce[m] [et] tenebras.

Moses first mentions the celestial bodies1 that God placed in the firmament so that they might shine in the heaven2 and illuminate the earth, namely the sun, moon, and stars3 with which the upper4 part of the world itself is adorned, just as the earth is adorned with the metals, plants and living beings5 that grow in it. After speaking about the nature of the firmament, it remained for him to speak about the workings of the stars6 and their function, to explain for what purpose they were created and for what task God assigned them.7

The two modes of action of celestial bodies8, movement and illumination, are universally recognizable, resulting in a twofold movement (I): One is (i) the entire world9, in which heaven and ether rotate in a perfect circle through the entire space of the universe10 in 24 hours. The other is the actual, multifaceted, and varied movement of (ii) the celestial bodies11, with the main movement of the sun, which passes through all the signs of the zodiac within twelve months. This creates the day, from which the daily cycles form the year. The remaining movements of the stars occur at different intervals. Rightly, Moses aptly and succinctly reminded us that the stars were placed in the firmament for days, years, and seasons.12

He also explicitly pointed out the other effect of the stars, which is illumination13 (II), when he said that they were arranged to shine in the heaven14 and to light up the earth. Thus, the bodies of the moon, the sun and the stars15 are distributed16 for this purpose17. Although the sun only apparently rises alone during the day (Cicero probably wants it to appear that only one sun is recognized)18, since it appears alone through the veiled stars19, it (the sun) is nevertheless the true and perfect light that illuminates everything with the greatest warmth and the brightest brilliance20. For although countless stars seem to twinkle and shine, those whose light is not full and clear neither provide warmth nor dispel the darkness through their multitude. Thus, two main elements can be found, (i) warmth and (ii) 'glory'21, which possess a diverse and at the same time cooperating power, wonderfully created by God for the preservation and creation of all things.22

However, this would require addressing very profound questions, each of which would require its own book: (i) In what way are these stars located in the firmament, whether as its nobler parts or as animals23 in their spheres, like fish in water or animals on earth.24 This point also required a discussion with (ii) astrologers25 regarding divination by the stars and the prediction of future events, which science confirmed by stating that Moses said the stars were placed by God as signs. Furthermore, (iii) the nature of the stars, their motion, their orbits, the spots26 on the moon, and the entire science of the stars (astronomy and astrology)27 must be addressed.28

Truly,

qua[m]q[uoniam] sint pulcra [et] digna cognitu.29

Maybe we will hear this Horace, but here was not the right place.30
