Foliu[m]/Blat VIIr

A contemporary English translation is presented from comparing the existing Latin, Early New High German (ENHG) and English (translation) texts for better comprehensibility. Especially since the ENHG text is difficult to understand compared to modern High German, as a profound knowledge of German (as a mother tongue) as well as already aknowledge of various dialects is required.

The first age of the world

When the original ancestors1 had transgressed God's commandment under the suggestion of the devil2 in the form of the serpent, God cursed them

et ait serpenti. Maledictus eris inter omnia anima[n]tia [et] bestias terre: super pectus tuum gradieris: et terram comedes cunctis diebus vite tue. Mulieri quoq[ue[ dixit. M[u]ltiplicabo eru[i]nas tuas: [et] co[n]ceptus tuos: in dolore paries filios [et] s[u]b viri potestate eris: [et] ip[s]e domi[n]abitur tibi. Ade v[er]o dixit Maledicta terra in opere tue i[n] laboribus comedes ex ea: spinas [et] tribulos germinabit tibi: in sudorevultus tui vesceris pane tuo: donec reuertaris in terram de qua sumptus es. Et cu[m] feciss[et] eis deus tunicas pelliceas eiecit eos de paradiso collocans ante illum cherubin cum flammeo gladio: vt viam ligni vite custodiat.3

Adam, the first human being formed from clay of the earth, was about thirty years old when his wife was given the name Eve. After he ate the fruit of the forbidden tree, which his wife offered him, they were cast out from the blissful4 Paradise into the accursed land5, according to the curse6 of the Lord God. Adam is to cultivate the earth by the sweat of his brow and feed himself on his own bread.

Eve too is to live in distress and bear children in pain; she whom the Lord has adorned with incomparable splendor7. However, the envious enemy of her happiness8 deceived her so that in feminine thoughtlessness she tasted the fruit of the tree with wicked desire9 and drew her husband to her will10. After putting on the leaf apron11, she was exiled12 from the Garden of Delights to the field of Hebron, together with her husband. Having frequently suffered the pains of childbirth, she aged through toil and labor and finally died, just as the Lord had foretold.
