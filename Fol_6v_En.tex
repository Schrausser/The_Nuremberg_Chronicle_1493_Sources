\hypertarget{foliumblat-viv}{%
\section{Foliu{[}m{]}/Blat VIv}\label{foliumblat-viv}}

A \emph{contemporary} English translation is presented from comparing
the existing Latin, Early New High German (ENHG) and English
(translation) texts for better comprehensibility. Especially since the
ENHG text is difficult to understand compared to modern High German, as
a \emph{profound} knowledge of German (as a mother tongue) as well as
already aknowledge of various \emph{dialects} is required.

\hypertarget{the-first-age-of-the-world}{%
\subsection{The first age of the
world}\label{the-first-age-of-the-world}}

According to the Hebrews, the first age of the world, from \emph{Adam}
to the \emph{Flood}, comprised 1656 years, while according to the
\textbf{Septuagint}, \textbf{Isidore} and many others, it was 2242
years, which consequently became the standard time reckoning.

The \emph{supreme goodness}\footnote{`SUmma bonitas' and `DIe höhst
  gůthait'.} wishes to impart this goodness to others, therefore it
created a rational being who would understand the supreme goodness and
thereby love it\footnote{`i{[}n{]}tellige{[}n{]}do amaret' and
  `versteende liebhet'.}, possess it {[}the supreme goodness{]} through
this love and be blessed\footnote{`beata' and `selig'.}.\footnote{from
  the `Sententiarum' (Petrus,
  \href{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-18241}{1484}, Book II, Chapter
  4). This formulation refers to a theological statement, often also
  attributed to Saint Augustine, which explains the purpose of the
  \emph{rational soul}: `Fecit \emph{Deus} rationalem creaturam ut
  summum bonum intelligeret, intelligendo amaret, amando possideret,
  possidendo frueretur.'}

God \emph{now}\footnote{`aut{[}em{]}' and `Aber'.} created the first man
by forming his body with the help of the angels from the clay of the
earth in the field of \emph{Damascus} and inspiring\footnote{`inspirauit'
  and `eingeystet'.} his \emph{face} with the \emph{breath of
life}\footnote{`spiraculum vite' and `geystung des lebens'.}, that
is\footnote{characteristic for the `Compendium historiae in genealogia
  Christi' (Poitiers,
  \href{https://libwww.freelibrary.org/digital/item/22446}{1499}).}, he
created a soul\footnote{`a{[}n{]}i{[}m{]}am' and `sel'.} which he united
with the formed body. Thus, man was created in his natural attributes in
the \emph{image}\footnote{`ymaginem' and `pildnus'.} of God and in his
spiritual gifts\footnote{`gratuitis' and `gnadenreichen dingen'.} in his
\emph{likeness}\footnote{`si{[}mi{]}litudinem' and `gleichnuß'.}.
Behold, the Lord bestows wonderful favor\footnote{`gra{[}tia{]}' and
  `gnad'.} upon us.

\begin{quote}
Cu{[}m{]} ergo mare{[}m{]} ad si{[}mi{]}litudine{[}m{]} sua{[}m{]}
primum finxisset: tu{[}m{]} etia{[}m{]} feminam {[}con{]}figurauit
adipsius hominis effigiem vt dou inter se p{[}er{]}mixti sex{[}us{]}
p{[}ro{]}pagare sobolem possent: {[}et{]} omne{[}m{]} terra{[}m{]}
multitudine opplere.\footnote{`So when he had first created man in his
  own image, he also created woman in the image of man, so that the two
  sexes, united, might produce offspring and fill the whole earth with
  multitudes', from the `Divine Institutes' (Lactantius,
  \href{https://books.google.com/books?id=qGsnghsi3uUC}{1497}, Book 2,
  Chapter 10).}
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
FOrmatis a{[}n{]}i{[}m{]}antib{[}us{]} terre {[}et{]} volatilib{[}us{]}
adduct{[}is{]} ad Ada{[}m{]} vt videret ea: cu{[}m{]} ada{[}m{]}
no{[}n{]} i{[}n{]}ueniret{[}ur{]} adiutor si{[}mi{]}lis sibi: immisit
d{[}omi{]}n{[}u{]}s sopore{[}m{]} in Ada{[}m{]} {[}et{]} tulis
una{[}m{]} de costis ei{[}us{]}: reple{[}n{]}s carne{[}m{]} pro ea:
{[}et{]} edificauit in muliere{[}m{]}. Qua{[}m{]} ada{[}m{]}
vide{[}n{]}s: dixit h{[}oc{]} nunc os de ossib{[}us{]} meis h{[}oc{]}
vocabit{[}ur{]} Issa q{[}uo{]}d lati{[}n{]}e m{[}u{]}l{[}ie{]}r
interp{[}re{]}tat{[}ur{]}: q{[}uorum{]} de viro su{[}m{]}pta
e{[}st{]}.\footnote{`After God had created the living creatures of the
  earth and the birds, he brought them to Adam so that he might see
  them. Since no suitable helper was found for Adam, the Lord caused
  Adam to fall into a deep sleep, took one of his ribs, filled it with
  flesh, and fashioned a woman from it. When Adam saw her, he said, This
  is now bone of my bone; she shall be called \emph{Issa}, which in
  Latin means woman, because she was taken from man', Hebrew
  \emph{Ishah} (woman) from \emph{Ish} (man), `Vulgata', Genesis 2:19-23
  (c.f. Jiménez de Cisneros,
  \href{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-46695}{1517}).}
\end{quote}

After God created Adam, he led him to Paradise and there made \emph{Eve}
from the sleeping man's \emph{rib}\footnote{`costa' and `ripp', Hebrew
  \emph{tsela} (side).}, thus forming him a companion. He made her
neither from the \emph{head}\footnote{`capite' and `haubt'.}, so that
she would not rule over the man, nor from the \emph{foot} of the man, so
that she would not be spurned, but from the \emph{side}\footnote{`latere'
  and `seyten'.}, so that the bond of love would prove
itself\footnote{`p{[}ro{]}baret{[}ur{]}' and `zu bewerung'.}.\footnote{from
  the `Sententiarum' (Petrus,
  \href{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-18241}{1484}, Book IV), a common
  motif in medieval scholasticism, see Thomas Aquinas
  (\href{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-15170}{1489}) or Hugh of
  St.~Victor
  (\href{https://archive.org/details/hugh-of-st.-victor-roy-j.-deferrari-on-the-sacraments-of-the-christian-faith-de-/mode/1up}{1951},).}

This also shows that it is not the value\footnote{`nobilitate' and
  `adel'.} of the place of origin, but one's own
\emph{goodness}\footnote{`virtute' and `tugend'.} that gives each
individual\footnote{`vnusq{[}ui{]}sq{[}ue{]}' and `ein yder'.} person
\emph{moral character}\footnote{`gra{[}tia{]}m' and `gnad'.}. The man,
created outside of Paradise, proves, although created in a \emph{lesser}
place\footnote{`inferiori loco' and `vndern stat'.}, to be \emph{better
for}\footnote{`melior inuenit{[}ur{]} p{[}ro{]}' and `besser gefunden
  für'.} Eve, who was created in Paradise.\footnote{from `De Paradiso'
  (Ambrosius,
  \href{https://books.google.com/books?id=L1DbnAAACAAJ}{1897}, 4.24), a
  common theological argument regarding the relationship between a
  person's place of origin and their moral character.}

God, the supreme creator of all things, thus created on the sixth day of
the world, on March 25th\footnote{In medieval theology, it was
  traditionally regarded as both the day of the creation of the world
  and the day of the crucifixion.}, after the animals of the earth, all
the reptiles and birds, \emph{Adam}, the first created\footnote{`p{[}ro{]}thoplastu{[}m{]}',
  a Latinized Greek term (protoplast).}, first man, from a red clod of
earth or clay\footnote{from the Hebrew word \emph{adamah} (Earth/soil).}
in the field of Damascus\footnote{According to medieval legends, the
  creation of Adam took place in a specific field near Damascus or
  Hebron.}, \emph{as the completion}\footnote{`fine{[}m{]}' and `ein
  end'.} \emph{and possessor}\footnote{`possessore{[}m{]}' and
  `besitzer'.} of all creatures.
